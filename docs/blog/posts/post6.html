Why Recovery to Current Market Price is Impossible

Why Recovery to Current Market Price is Impossible

Published: December 31, 2025

One of the most common misconceptions among cryptocurrency investors is that buying more during a price decline can eventually bring their average cost, or break-even price, down to the current market level. This belief leads to frustration when tools refuse to provide a finite solution for full recovery.

The truth lies in basic mathematics: when you buy additional coins at the current market price, the new break-even price can approach that market price but can never reach or fall below it, no matter how much you invest.

The Mathematical Proof

Suppose your current break-even is higher than the market price. Any new purchases are made at the lower market price, pulling the average downward. However, the existing higher-cost position continues to exert upward pressure on the average.

As additional investment increases, the weight of the new lower-price coins grows, and the break-even moves closer to the market price. Yet because the original position remains fixed, the average is always a weighted combination of the old higher price and the new lower price.

This creates an asymptotic relationship: the break-even approaches the market price as investment approaches infinity, but with any finite amount, it remains strictly above the market price.

A Simple Example

Imagine holding one coin with a break-even of sixty thousand dollars while the market price is forty thousand. Buying another coin at forty thousand brings the new break-even to fifty thousand dollars exactly halfway between the two prices.

Buying ten more at forty thousand yields a break-even around forty-five thousand four hundred fifty-five dollars. Buying one hundred more drops it to about forty-nine thousand five hundred four dollars. No matter how many you add, the original sixty thousand dollar position prevents reaching forty thousand.

Key Implications

  • Full recovery to current price requires infinite capital
  • Very high recovery percentages need disproportionately large investments
  • Partial recovery targets are far more practical
  • Understanding this prevents overcommitment during downturns

Practical Takeaway

Rather than chasing the impossible goal of matching current market price, investors should target meaningful partial reductions, perhaps seventy-five or ninety percent of the gap. These achieve substantial improvement with reasonable additional capital.

Recognizing this mathematical limit helps maintain realistic expectations and sound risk management when averaging down in declining markets.

This fundamental principle applies to any asset class and remains true regardless of position size or price levels involved.
