Behind the Recovery Simulations: Iterative Optimization

Behind the Recovery Simulations: Iterative Optimization

Published: December 31, 2025

The recovery tool appears simple on the surface, requiring only four inputs to produce a precise recovery strategy. What users do not see is the sophisticated iterative optimization running silently in the background to deliver those accurate results.

At its core, the challenge is mathematical: given a current position and desired new break-even price, determine exactly how much additional capital must be invested at the current market price to achieve that target. There is no simple algebraic formula that solves this directly, so the tool employs a proven numerical method known as binary search.

How Binary Search Works in This Context

Binary search is an efficient algorithm typically used to find items in sorted lists, but it excels at solving equations where a direct solution is unavailable. Here, it treats additional investment amount as the variable to optimize.

The process begins by establishing a reasonable search range, from zero investment up to a conservatively high upper bound. It then repeatedly tests the midpoint of this range, calculating what the new break-even price would be if that amount were invested.

If the resulting break-even is too high, the algorithm knows the correct amount must be larger and adjusts the lower bound upward. If too low, it raises the upper bound. With each iteration, the range halves, rapidly converging on the precise investment needed.

Why This Approach Is Ideal

This method guarantees convergence to the correct answer within a tiny tolerance, usually in fewer than fifty iterations regardless of position size. It is both fast and reliable, completing almost instantly even on modest devices.

Advantages Over Alternatives

  • Extremely high precision without complex math libraries
  • No risk of infinite loops thanks to bounded iterations
  • Works consistently across all valid input ranges
  • Handles edge cases naturally through early checks

From Theory to Practice

Consider wanting to reduce your break-even from sixty thousand to fifty thousand dollars when the market price is forty thousand. The algorithm quickly determines that investing another forty thousand dollars achieves exactly fifty thousand as the new average.

Each test during the search evaluates the weighted average formula with the trial investment, guiding the next guess. The result is not an approximation but the mathematically exact amount, rounded only for display purposes.

This behind-the-scenes optimization ensures that every recovery recommendation is trustworthy and actionable, giving investors confidence in their averaging-down decisions during challenging market conditions.
